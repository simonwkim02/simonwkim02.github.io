Pre-Contact Perception for Multifinger Grasping

Integrating Time-of-Flight Proximity Sensing into a π0.5 Vision–Language–Action Policy
Simon Won-Jin Kim (김원진) · KAIST · Master's Thesis Defense

Research Summary

Objective: integrate the multizone TMF8829 ToF sensor as a complementary input to a π0.5 VLA model.
Hypothesis: proximity improves grasping where vision-only VLAs fail — precision grasps of small / thin objects.
Metrics: success rate of the VPLA model versus a vision-only baseline.
• Pick-and-place of small / thin objects (chopstick only), scored as a per-trial funnel: approach → pre-contact pose → grasp/contact → pick-and-place.

Contributions

  1. Integration of multizone ToF sensing into an existing VLA architecture (late fusion at the π0.5 action expert).
  2. An ablation of various VPLA models against a vision-only baseline on the evaluation task.

Why these models? — the ablation story

Each model was chosen to answer the question the previous one raised. Read top to bottom:

  1. Vision-only π0.5 (VLA baseline) — Does vision alone suffice? It establishes the failure mode: in the last few centimeters the closing fingers occlude the chopstick and the wrist camera loses depth, so a vision-only policy mis-judges the final approach. → motivates adding proximity.
  2. VLPA late fusion, no dropout — Does adding proximity help? The policy over-relies on the high-rate proximity stream: offline it is strongly proximity-conditioned, but in closed loop it jitters in place and never approaches. → motivates balancing the modalities.
  3. Modality-dropout sweep (proximity-drop pp = 0, 0.3, 0.5, and 0.2) — How hard should we force vision vs. proximity? High pp → proximity ignored; no dropout → over-relied; pp = 0.2 is the balanced point (vision drives the approach, proximity conditions the pre-contact grasp). → identifies the balanced recipe.
  4. Over-training check — Is the balance stable? Even at pp = 0.2, training longer keeps raising proximity reliance during the approach, so the best closed-loop checkpoint is an intermediate one. Dropout balances the modalities probabilistically but cannot structurally keep proximity out of the approach. → motivates gating.
  5. VLPA-Gated — Can we structurally confine proximity to where it helps? The proposed fix, and the model evaluated live below.

What “gated” means, and how I implemented it

The problem it solves: proximity is exactly what we want at pre-contact (it measures the depth the occluded camera can't), but during the far-field approach the signal is mostly empty/saturated and the policy learns to lean on it — mis-positioning the hand before the grasp.

Gating: the proximity input is held at a neutral “nothing-near” value during the approach and switched to the real signal only once a surface is close (pre-contact). So vision owns the approach; proximity sharpens only the final grasp — the division of labor the dropout model could only approximate.

Implementation: the gate lives in the data pipeline and is keyed on proximity closeness (it opens once a sufficient fraction of valid sensor zones report a surface within a near-distance band, ~100 mm). Because it runs in the data pipeline, the model is trained on gated proximity — it never sees real proximity during the approach — so the same gate at inference is in-distribution, not a perturbation. No modality dropout is used (gating is the lever). The gate is a fixed near-distance threshold; a soft ramp at the hand-off and a learned contact-imminence gate are future work.

How the validation tests work

Two layers. The offline diagnostics ask “does the policy actually use proximity?”; the live trials ask “does it actually grasp?”

Offline proximity-conditioning diagnostics (computed on held-out frames):

  • Controlled-noise ratio ρ (the headline test): feed the policy the same frame twice — once with real proximity, once with neutral proximity — while holding the decoder's internal randomness fixed, and measure how much the predicted action changes, divided by the action's own noise floor. ρ ≈ 1 = proximity ignored; ρ ≫ 1 = over-relied; ρ moderately > 1 = balanced.
  • Phase-split: separate frames into approach vs. pre-contact and confirm the intended division of labor — vision dominates the approach, proximity matters at pre-contact.
  • Approach-robustness (swing): during the approach, perturb proximity to an extreme “very close” value; if the action barely moves (swing < 1) the approach is robust to a bad proximity reading.

Live per-trial funnel (the table below):

Each of the 20 trials per variant is scored on four stages, where each stage requires the previous: approach (reach the chopstick) → pre-contact pose (hand positioned & oriented to grasp) → grasp/contact (close on it) → pick-and-place (lift & place). Because it's nested, the table shows exactly where each model breaks and which modality owns that stage — e.g. the vision-only baseline reaching the object but failing the pre-contact pose, which is the stage proximity is meant to fix.
